# SC pre-publication rounds — conductor

This file governs work on the Thai translation of *Steps to Christ* in th/SC. It is not loaded automatically; read it in full before running any part of this procedure. The repository root CLAUDE.md governs reporting, register, Thai text handling and concurrency, and is not restated here. Brian's rulings are written into this file in the same reply that receives them, one unwrapped line each; the queue of deferred decisions is th/SC/04_assets/planning/SIDEQUESTS.md and nothing else answers what is open.

## 1. Trigger

1.A. "pre SCNN" — the pre-processing round of section 4 on a raw chapter in 01_raw, which finishes the translator's draft: every parenthesis resolved, the header repaired, citations converted, and the QA1 classes marked in the same pass. SC12 finished its pre round on 21 August with three parentheses standing for the editors at {SC 106.2}, {SC 110.1} and {SC 111.3}; SC13's pre round was mid-way on branch SC12-pre-edit at that date, so confirm which copy of SC13 is current before dispatching.
1.B. "qa1 SCNN [SCNN ...]" — round one, accuracy, under section 3.C. Several chapters named in one trigger run in parallel, each as its own chain, because they are separate files; the batches inside a chapter run sequentially because marker numbers depend on order.
1.C. "qa2 SCNN [SCNN ...]" — round two, terms and clarity, under section 3.D. It runs only after QA1's resolutions have landed in the chapter and the glossary of 2.C carries adjudicated rows for the chapter's terms.
1.D. "qa3 SCNN" — round three, the reader pass of 3.E. Its agent is not written yet; say so and stop.
1.E. "check SCNN" — the translator has finished resolving markers; run the post-resolution sweep of section 5.
1.F. "terms SCNN [SCNN ...]" — dispatch th-glossary-miner over the named chapters under section 7, then relay its rows for adjudication.
1.G. A term or corpus question — answer it yourself with grep over th/PP, th/MB, th/SJ and th/SC, copying every Thai form out of a file and never retyping one. There is no Thai formfind script yet, so group raw matches by their surrounding words before quoting a count, because a short form matches across word boundaries.
1.H. Anything else — ask rather than guess.
1.I. "7/3/1" and its "model: X/Y/Z" variants — the drill of root CLAUDE.md 3.C: weigh up to X candidates and never pad toward X, show the top Y ranked, write the top Z into the marker as its proposal. Fable at xhigh and 7/3/1 are the defaults. Stacked parentheses (6.D) and an inline [[fable: ...]] note call for the same drill.

## 2. Paths

2.A. Chapter under work: chapters live in th/SC/01_raw, th/SC/02_edit or th/SC/03_public depending on maturity, so confirm the stage with ls rather than assuming. SC01–SC09 are in 02_edit, SC10 and SC11 in 03_public, SC12 and SC13 in 01_raw.
2.B. English source: th/SC/00_source/SCNN_en.md, anchored with "## {SC ###.#}" headings; the same files sit at source/SC/ at the repository root, and the scripts of 2.E look in both places. SC04_en.md carries one heading for eleven paragraphs, so the scripts also anchor an English paragraph by the "{SC ###.#}" tag that closes it, and a batch cutting its range out of that file must do the same.
2.C. Governing files, shared by every Thai project: th/assets/translation_profile/thai-glossary.txt and thai-profile.txt. Both are being built — a row enters only after the translator adjudicates it from a mining run — and a row is silent until its Notes cell carries [CHECK] or [FLAG], as the glossary's own header says. While they are thin, term-consistency evidence still comes from grepping the finished Thai translations — th/PP (03_public plus PP31–43 in 02_edit), th/MB (published), th/SJ (03_public, no English source) and the finished SC chapters — and is evidence, never a verdict. Part 1 of lo/assets/translation_profile/profile.txt is the language-agnostic translation philosophy and binds this project; every later part of that file is Lao-only.
2.D. Session outputs: ~/claude-sandbox/sc-audit/ — scNN-companion.md, scNN-term-candidates.txt, scNN-report.md, the resolution sheet, and nothing in the repository.
2.E. Scripts live in th/SC/04_assets/scripts/ and are invoked by that exact relative path from the repository root so the permission allow-rules match. Three belong to the rounds and none duplicates another; sc_common.py is their shared parser and is never run on its own.

    sc_punctcheck.py        the mechanical Thai sweep of 3.B over a chapter or a ref range; run by the batch auditor on every batch and by you at check SCNN
    sc_refcheck.py          the scripture-citation check of 3.B: every citation in the Thai paragraph against the English paragraph's citations, plus book name, chapter and verse bounds and the version label; book names are rows of th_books.txt beside it
    sc_resolution_sheet.py  the marker sheet and integrity count you read before reporting, per 4.E

2.F. The queue of deferred decisions: th/SC/04_assets/planning/SIDEQUESTS.md, under root CLAUDE.md 3.B.
2.G. Compile check. From th/SC, ./typst-custom compile --root . <stage>/SCNN_th.typ $TMPDIR/SCNN.pdf proves a chapter still compiles; run it at check SCNN, never on a chapter holding markers, because markers are not Typst.

## 3. The rounds

3.A. Three light rounds rather than one heavy one, ruled by Brian on 11 September 2026: each round marks one kind of thing, so that he is never asked to weigh word choices while he is checking facts. A finding outside the round's classes is not marked and not reported; it waits for the round that owns it, and the batch auditor's own file says so.
3.B. Mechanical sweep, every round, every batch, by script: sc_punctcheck.py for Thai digits (U+0E50–U+0E59), zero-width and other invisible characters, doubled spaces, unbalanced quotation marks, a paragraph whose "// {SC ###.#}" comment and "#EGW[\{SC ###.#\}]" tag disagree, Latin letters outside a citation, and Typst comments holding working notes; sc_refcheck.py for every scripture citation. Both print findings and not candidates, and every finding takes a marker; the lines sc_refcheck.py prefixes NOTE (a citation the Thai carries beyond the English, a THSV label) are not findings and take none. A THSV label is redundant under 6.E, and the batch auditor deletes every one in its range silently as the chapter runs, in every round; the conductor's report carries one NOTE per chapter giving the count and the anchors. Ruled by the translator on 11 September; no chapter is edited for this outside a run.
3.C. QA1, accuracy. Classes: SPELL (a Thai typo, a digit or invisible-character defect), GRAM (a Thai grammar error, counted as a typo, ruled 11 September), REF (a citation whose book, chapter, verse, extent or version label is wrong, or that the English cites and the Thai lacks), NOTE (a footnote missing, extra or pointing at the wrong place), FACT (a fact that differs: actor, number, direction, name, negation, inverted truth value, a scope narrowed or widened), OMISSION (English content absent at clause level or larger), ADDITION (Thai content the English lacks at clause level or larger), ALIGN (paragraph boundaries that disagree with the English). No TERM, no CLARITY, no EDIT, and CHOICE only where a translator parenthesis survives in a chapter past the pre round. SPELL, GRAM, REF and NOTE carry no severity, because each is fixed or dismissed at a glance; FACT, OMISSION, ADDITION and ALIGN carry HIGH or MED, and LOW is never written in this round.
3.D. QA2, terms and clarity. Classes: TERM (a rendering that contradicts a ruled glossary row, or renders one English term two ways inside the chapter without a reason the passage gives), CLARITY (a wrong reading a Thai reader could land on, named in one sentence). An accuracy finding QA1 missed is still marked in its QA1 class. The glossary guides and never rules; the argument for a TERM marker comes from the passage, and variation within a term family is accepted unless there is a positive reason to narrow it. QA2 runs on a chapter only after its QA1 markers are resolved and check SCNN has passed.
3.E. QA3, the reader pass. Fable reads every paragraph QA1 and QA2 changed, whole, and judges whether the paragraph now reads as natural Thai and carries the English, writing REVERT, REWORD or FIX markers only where a change should not stand. The reader agent and its packet script are written when QA2 has run on a first chapter, so that the design rests on what QA1 and QA2 actually changed; the GC model is gc-qa3-reader and lo/GC/04_assets/scripts/gc_qa3_packet.py on the GC-instructions branch.

## 4. Chapter procedure, every round

4.A. Preflight. Confirm 2.A and 2.B exist for the chapter. If the chapter already contains [[ markers, stop and say which numbers remain; the translator resolves markers between rounds. Check cleanliness with git status --short -- th/SC, read per root section 4: your chapter's file must be unmodified, another chapter's file is never a reason to stop. Do not read the chapter body into your own context; grep only.
4.B. Size and split exactly as the GC rule: wc -w on the English source, batch count is that word count divided by 2200 rounded up, a chapter under 2700 English words is one batch, cut at anchors so no remainder piles onto the last batch. State the split and proceed without waiting for approval. SC chapters run 1,400 to 4,100 English words, so every chapter is one or two batches.
4.C. Dispatch sc-batch-auditor per batch, sequentially, never in parallel: marker numbering depends on order. Each dispatch states chapter NN, its stage directory, ref range, starting marker number, the round name (pre, qa1 or qa2), and whether it is the first batch. Markers restart at #1 in every round of a chapter, because a round begins only on a chapter clear of markers. The next batch's starting number is the previous batch's reported last number plus one; a batch that wrote nothing leaves the number unchanged.
4.D. After the last batch, dispatch sc-run-check with the chapter number, its stage directory, the round name, the expected total marker count — the last number reported by the last batch that wrote one — and the scoping paragraph naming which files under th/SC belong to other chapters. If no batch wrote a marker, skip the run-check and say the chapter came back clean.
4.E. Run python3 th/SC/04_assets/scripts/sc_resolution_sheet.py --chapter NN before you report and read its summary line: intact markers, damaged markers, unterminated brackets, and markers whose old side is not in the committed chapter. That last count means an agent typed a span from memory instead of copying it, so delete those markers and establish the finding again. The sheet it writes under ~/claude-sandbox/sc-audit/ is where Brian reads every marker beside its full English paragraph, so give him its path.
4.F. Report per the root CLAUDE.md report shape. The reference mark is the {SC ###.#} anchor plus the marker number. Report the counts by class from scNN-report.md, one NOTE with the count and anchors of THSV labels deleted under 3.B, any run-check failures, every DECIDE, and any item whose effect reaches past its own site; a decision confined to one marker is settled at the cursor and is not repeated in the report.

## 5. Post-resolution sweep ("check SCNN")

5.A. Grep the chapter for [[ first; standing markers end the check, so name their numbers and stop.
5.B. When the chapter is clear, run sc_punctcheck.py --chapter NN and sc_refcheck.py --chapter NN yourself and run the compile check of 2.G. A finding from any of the three is reported with its anchor and written into the chapter as a [[FIX #N|old -> new|note]] marker, numbered onward from the round's last number.
5.C. Then dispatch sc-resolve-check with the chapter number, its stage directory, the last marker number used so far, and every resolved marker's class, anchor, old span and note, which its 5.E needs and cannot recover from the file. Relay its report verbatim, because it carries Thai spans. A PASS means the chapter is clean to commit and ready to upload to Google Docs.
5.D. A choice-parenthesis the translator has ruled to stand for the editors is reported as a NOTE naming that ruling, never as a defect.

## 6. Rules

6.A. You orchestrate; agents work. Do not audit paragraphs or edit the chapter yourself. A run's only repo edit is markers in the chapter file; every other artifact goes to ~/claude-sandbox/sc-audit/.
6.B. Chapter files are Typst. Each paragraph carries a "// {SC ###.#}" comment above it and an "#EGW[\{SC ###.#\}]" tag at its end; footnotes are inline "#footnote[...]". Reference prose by anchor, never by line number. A chapter holding markers must not be added to book.typ and must not be compiled, because markers are not Typst.
6.C. Thai text rules. Western digits only, per root 5.A. No invisible characters, per root 5.B — the Thai corpus carries none and Typst does its own Thai line breaking. Never transliterate, per root 5.C. Thai sentences do not close with a period and questions usually carry no question mark; spaces mark phrase and sentence boundaries. Thai does not space around และ the way Lao spaces its conjunctions: และ stays closed on both sides unless the clause it joins is long. Ruled by the translator on 18 August. None of the Lao punctuation rules in lo/GC/CLAUDE.md section 5 carries over, and gc_punctcheck.py must not be run on Thai files.
6.D. Parentheses in a raw or edit-stage chapter are the translator's own unresolved word choices: (A/B) offers alternatives, (word) marks a tentative word or phrase. Resolving every one is pre-round work, never something to leave standing, except where the translator rules that a specific choice goes to the editors — that parenthesis stands for the round and travels to Google Docs. Parentheses holding a scripture citation are ordinary text, not choices. Multiple layers of parentheses — (phrase))) and the like — are the translator's signal that he doubts the wording is the best possible and wants extra effort: run the 1.I drill on the site. His raw notes in the text — an inline [[fable: ...]] tag, a (before: .../after: ...) pair, a bracketed question — are questions addressed to whoever meets them: answer the question, fold the note into a marker carrying concrete wording, and never read them as manuscript text or count them defects.
6.E. Scripture citations, ruled by the translator on 16 August. The book is migrating citations from footnotes to inline: a citation sits in parentheses wherever it fits the flow of the sentence, sometimes at the paragraph end; where a parenthesis would break the flow it is worked into the prose ("as ยอห์น 3:16 says...") or left as a footnote. The default Bible version is THSV — an unlabelled citation means THSV, so a THSV label is redundant and the batch auditor deletes it silently under 3.B. The mechanical footnote-to-inline conversion is made silently with no marker in the pre round only, because the translator cherry-picks from the diff; in QA1 and QA2 a footnote citation is left where it stands, and only a genuine question of placement or flow gets a marker.
6.F. Header repair, pre round only. The raw chapters lack the #import and #show: apply-styles lines every edit-stage chapter carries; the first batch of a pre round inserts them silently, matching the 02_edit shape with proofing: true and the run date.
6.G. Line breaking is never fixed in a manuscript. Thai break points live centrally in th/SC/04_assets/template/dictionary.typ — protected words and soft-hyphen hints applied at typeset time — so manuscripts stay free of soft hyphens and a bad break is fixed by a dictionary entry. th/SJ carries the most developed template and dictionary and is the model when SC's typesetting is brought up to date; that is entry 3 of the queue and belongs to typesetting rather than to these rounds.
6.H. Every agent carries its model and effort in its own definition, so name the agent in the dispatch and do not override either.
6.I. Context you load is the dominant cost of a run. Grep rather than read, pass an agent what you already know, and gather corpus evidence as lines, never as whole files.
6.J. Git state and GitHub are the translator's alone. Agents may read but never change either — no staging, committing, pushing, PRs or merges, and no offering to; hand him the commands to run himself.
6.K. A definite reference may be named for the reader. English "the" before a referent the author trusted her 19th-century readers to know — the psalmist, the wise man, the Reformation — licenses naming it for today's Thai reader: กษัตริย์ดาวิด for the psalmist of Psalm 37, King Solomon for the wise man. Name only what is certain, decide at the site by what the sentence needs, and let no glossary row outrank the reader's understanding — the reader must feel and understand; this is a book, not a textbook.
6.L. Never apply a fix across chapters on your own initiative, however plainly a ruling implies it. What you owe the translator unprompted is the proposal with the evidence he decides from: how many sites, in which chapters, what changes at each, and which of those files another session has modified.

## 7. Glossary building ("terms")

7.A. The glossary and profile of 2.C are built between QA1 and QA2, from the term-candidates files the QA1 batches leave under 2.D and from mining runs: "terms SCNN [SCNN ...]" dispatches th-glossary-miner over the named chapters with an output file under 2.D, and its five sections — terms, proper nouns, register, Bible versions, spelling variants — come back as paste-ready rows and numbered observations. The 16 August run over SC12 and SC13 is at ~/claude-sandbox/sc-audit/sc12-13-term-mine.md and is input to the next run, not repeated.
7.B. Relay the miner's rows to the translator in chunks of 15 heads, each head with its English, every Thai form found with a count and one {SC ###.#} or {PP ###.#} ref per form, and no recommendation unless he asks for one. He rules a chunk; the ruled rows go into the governing files of 2.C in the same reply, with [CHECK] or [FLAG] in the Notes cell where he wants the row enforced, and nothing goes there before he rules.
7.C. A row carries the English head, the Thai form or forms, and a Notes cell of at most 15 words saying what an agent must do; counts, ref lists and reasoning stay in the miner's output file, never in the row.

8.A. The translator's editor and reviewers work in Google Docs while the repository stays authoritative. ## 9. Reporting specifics

9.A. The root CLAUDE.md report shape governs; in detail blocks the TH: label replaces LO:.
9.B. Front the decision. A DECIDE detail block opens with the decision to be made and the recommendation in its first sentence, and every labelled part (ISSUE, FIX1, FIX2) is separated from the next by a blank line, never run together into one block of text.
9.C. In the summary list, bold each line's number and label — as in **1. DECIDE** — so the eye can jump down the list.
9.D. One agent writes for the translator directly and is relayed verbatim because it returns Thai spans: sc-resolve-check. sc-batch-auditor, sc-run-check and th-glossary-miner write for you, and you compose the translator's report from their numbered items, copying every Thai form from their files rather than retyping it.
