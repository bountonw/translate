
# Chapter 1: Ye^lu^saa^lem Zingh Zuqc Mietc

### Ziouv Yesu weic Ye^lu^saa^lem Zingh Nzauh Nyiemv

Ziouv Yesu caux Ninh nyei ziepc nyeic dauh sai-gorx hungx jienv Ye^lu^saa^lem Zingh mingh. Taux Ga'Lanv Mbong, Ziouv paaiv sai-gorx mbuo mingh zorqv maaz-li dorn. Maaih mienh camv haic ndutc-ndutc nyei cuotv daih zipv Ninh. Yesu geh jienv maaz-li dorn. “Mienh camv nyei longc ganh nyei domh lui dimc wuov jauv. Maaih deix yaac mingh ndeic gaeqv ndiangx-normh yietc dorv yietc dorv nyei, dimc jienv jauv. Mingh ndaangc wuov deix, caux nqa'haav gan jienv mingh wuov deix, lomh nzoih heuc, ‘Ho^saa^naa! Tov Tin-Hungh ceix fuqv dengv Ziouv nyei mbuox daaih wuov dauh. Tov Tin-Hungh ceix fuqv bun meih oix liepc nyei guoqv, se mbuo nyei ong-taaix, Ndaawitv Hungh, nyei guoqv. Ho^saa^naa! Ceng yiem hlang jiex wuov dauh Tin-Hungh.’” (Maako 11:8–10.)

Yangh jauv taux mbong-ningv, ninh mbuo ziouc dingh. Ziouv Yesu mangc njiec buatc Ye^lu^saa^lem Zingh yiem ndiev maengx. Wuov zanc doix zuqc Yiutai Mienh jiex Siex Maengc Zipv nyei ziangh hoc. Maaih Yiutai Mienh yiem ganh norm deic bung daaih camv nyei weic jiex zipv. Ninh mbuo nyei ndopv-liuh buangv nzengc ndau-kungx nyei dorngx. Ziouv buatc Ye^lu^saa^lem Zingh weih gormx nyei mbong maaih lingh maaih ndeic caux a'ngunc huingx mbaih jienv hnangv domh tei nyei zaux-kaqv; buatc zingh nyei domh laatc youc hoz youc hlang, Hungh Dinc nzueic nyei, mba'hnoi a'lamh deix muotv yaac ziux zuqc zaangc Tin-Hungh nyei biauv youc! youc wuov. Ye^lu^saa^lem zingh hnangv Ging-Sou nyei waac bungx waac-maux gorngv, ‘Yie zoux hungh maac zueiz jienv weic, yie maiv zeiz auv-guaav, yietc liuz maiv zuqc nzauh nyiemv.’ (Laauc Yaangh 18:7.)

Wuov zanc zuangx miengh lomh nzoih a'hneiv jienv ceng Tin-Hungh, lomh nzoih haiz njien-youh orn-lorqc, mv baac Ziouv Yesus nyiemv. Maiv zeiz weix Ninh ganh nyei kouv naanc nyiemv. Maiv zeiz weic mienh maanh nyei zuiz hniev, dongh Ninh oix ndaam weic fiqv mienh nyei zuiz. Maiv zeiz weic Ninh ganh oix zuqc diev mun, dongh ninh oix zuqc ding jienv yiem ziepc nzaangc jaax. Mv baac Yesu weic Ye^lu^saa^lem Zingh oix zuqc mietc nyei camv-cin camv-waanc nyei mienh nyiemv. Yesu youh nzauh mba-nziu yuqc maiv zeiz weic ninh ganh, mv baac weic Ye^lu^saa^lem Zingh nyei baeqc fingx. 

Ninh gornh, “Feix duqv ih hnoi meih hiuv duqv haaix nyungc haih bun meih maaih baengh orn. Mv baac ih zanc naaiv deix sic gem jienv, meih nyei m'zing mangc maiv buatc. Ziangh hoc oix taux, meih nyei win-wangv oix wetv nie-ndui huing gormx meih. Ninh mbuo oix weic jienv biei bung kuonx jienv meih. Liemh meih ganh caux yiem meih nyei zingh laatc gu'nyuoz nyei mienh yietc zungv ninh mbuo oix mietc nzengc, maiv liouh yietc kuaaiv la'bieiv lapv jienv, weic zuqc meih zieqv maiv duqv Tin-Hungh daaih njoux meih nyei ziangh hoc.” (Lugaa 19:42-44)

### A History of Rejection

Jesus looked back at more than a thousand years of God’s care for this chosen people. He saw Mount Moriah, where the young Isaac had been bound to the altar—a symbol of the offering of the Son of God. He saw the place where the covenant of blessing had been confirmed to Abraham. Jerusalem had been honored by God above all the earth. Here, prophets had spoken their warnings. Here, the blood of lambs had been offered daily, pointing forward to the Lamb of God. If Israel had remained loyal to Heaven, Jerusalem would have stood forever. {GC 18.2}

But the history of this favored people was a record of rebellion. They had abused their privileges and rejected God’s grace. God had loved them with more than a father’s pity, sending messengers to plead with them. When they rejected these warnings, He sent the best gift of heaven—His own Son. Jesus had come to plead with the city. He had brought them out of Egypt and planted them as a goodly vine (Psalm 80:8), but when He looked for fruit, He found only wild grapes (Isaiah 5:1-4). {GC 19.1, 19.2}

For three years, the Lord of light had walked among His people. He went about doing good, healing the sick, setting captives free, and preaching the gospel to the poor. He issued the gracious call: "Come unto Me, all ye that labor and are heavy-laden, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). Though they repaid His love with hatred, He remained steadfast in His mission of mercy. But now, the hour of hope was passing. The cup of God’s wrath was almost full. {GC 20.1, 20.3}

### The Vision of Doom

From the Mount of Olives, Jesus looked into the future. He saw the city surrounded by alien armies. He heard the tread of soldiers gathering for war. He heard the voices of mothers and children crying for bread in the besieged city. He saw the holy temple in flames, leaving only a heap of smoldering ruins. {GC 21.1}

His love and pity found a voice in the mournful words: "O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets... how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not!" (Matthew 23:37). He spoke a prophecy of their destruction: "Thine enemies shall cast a trench about thee... and shall lay thee even with the ground, and thy children within thee" (Luke 19:43-44). {GC 17.1, 21.2}

### The Temple and the Warning

Later, the disciples looked with awe at the temple. It had been built with massive blocks of white marble, enriched with gifts from emperors. They called Jesus' attention to it, saying, "See what manner of stones and what buildings are here!" (Mark 13:1). Jesus replied with a solemn prophecy: "Verily I say unto you, There shall not be left here one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down" (Matthew 24:2). {GC 24.3, 25.1}

The disciples were confused. They associated the destruction of Jerusalem with the end of the world. They asked, "When shall these things be? and what shall be the sign of Thy coming, and of the end of the world?" (Matthew 24:3). Jesus’ answer had two meanings: it foretold the destruction of Jerusalem and the terrors of the last great day. {GC 25.2, 25.3}

Jesus gave them a specific sign to watch for. He said, "When ye therefore shall see the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, stand in the holy place... then let them which be in Judea flee into the mountains" (Matthew 24:15-16). This sign would be the idolatrous Roman standards set up in the holy ground extending outside the city walls. When this happened, escape must be instant. Those on the housetop must not go down to save their treasures; those in the fields must not return for their clothes. {GC 25.4}

### Signs and Wonders

For forty years, God delayed His judgment. Wonderful was the long-suffering of God. But the people hardened their hearts. Finally, the protection of God was withdrawn, and the nation was left to the control of Satan. The leaders were ruled by impulse and blind rage. They became Satanic in their cruelty. {GC 27.3, 28.1}

Strange signs appeared before the disaster. A supernatural light shone over the temple at night. Chariots and soldiers gathering for battle were seen in the clouds at sunset. Priests in the sanctuary felt the earth tremble and heard voices saying, "Let us depart hence." The massive eastern gate, which required twenty men to close, opened on its own at midnight. {GC 29.3}

### The Escape of the Christians

Not one Christian perished in the destruction of Jerusalem. They had watched for the sign Jesus promised. The Roman army under Cestius surrounded the city. The Jews, despairing of resistance, were ready to surrender. But unexpectedly, the Roman general withdrew his forces without any apparent reason. This was God’s providence. {GC 30.2}

The Jewish forces rushed out to attack the retreating Romans. While both armies were fully engaged in battle, the Christians had the opportunity to leave the city. The country was clear of enemies. They fled without delay to a place of safety—the city of Pella, in the land of Perea. {GC 30.2, 31.1}

### The Siege and Famine

Later, the Romans returned under Titus. The city was surrounded during the Passover, trapping millions of Jews inside. The stores of food had already been destroyed by the fighting factions within the city. Terrible famine followed. A measure of wheat was sold for a talent. Men gnawed on the leather of their belts and sandals. {GC 31.2}

The prophecy of Moses, given fourteen centuries earlier, was fulfilled: "The tender and delicate woman among you... her eye shall be evil toward... her children... for she shall eat them for want of all things" (Deuteronomy 28:56-57). Mothers snatched food from their own parents, and some even killed and ate their own children. {GC 32.1}

The Romans tried to terrorize the Jews into surrender. Prisoners were whipped and crucified before the walls. Hundreds were put to death daily until there was no room left for crosses. The awful prayer uttered at the trial of Christ was visited upon them: "His blood be on us, and on our children" (Matthew 27:25). {GC 32.2}

### The Burning of the Temple

Titus wished to save the magnificent temple. He appealed to the Jewish leaders not to defile the holy place with blood, but they answered with curses. Titus decided to take the temple by storm but ordered his soldiers not to burn it. However, his commands were disregarded. {GC 32.3, 33.1}

In the fury of battle, a soldier threw a firebrand through an opening in the porch. The cedar-lined chambers caught fire. Titus rushed to the place and commanded the soldiers to quench the flames, but they did not hear him. Blinding smoke and fire forced the officers to retreat, and the noble building was left to its fate. {GC 33.1, 33.2}

The whole summit of the hill blazed like a volcano. The shouts of the Roman soldiers mingled with the roar of the fire and the wailing of the people. Thousands perished in the flames or by the sword. {GC 34.1, 35.1}

### The Lesson for the World

The city and the temple were razed to their foundations. The ground was "plowed like a field" (Jeremiah 26:18). More than a million people perished; the survivors were carried away as captives or sold as slaves. The Jews had filled the cup of vengeance themselves; their destruction was the harvest they had sown. {GC 35.2, 35.3}

The Saviour’s prophecy concerning Jerusalem is to have another fulfillment. The fate of the chosen city is a shadow of the doom of a world that rejects God’s mercy. Terrible were the results of rejecting the authority of Heaven in Jerusalem, but the results in the last days will be even more terrible. {GC 36.2}

But God’s people will be delivered, everyone found written in the book of life (Isaiah 4:3). Christ will come the second time to gather His faithful ones. "Then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory" (Matthew 24:30). {GC 37.1}

We must not neglect the lesson. As Jesus warned His disciples of Jerusalem’s destruction so they could escape, He has warned the world of the day of final destruction. He declares, "Watch ye therefore... lest coming suddenly He find you sleeping" (Mark 13:35-36). To those who do not watch, the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night, and they shall not escape (1 Thessalonians 5:2-3). {GC 37.2, 38.1}
