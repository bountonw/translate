# GC open terms — decision history

Why this file exists. gc-batch-auditor 2.B reads GC-open-terms.md for every
ref in a batch's range, so that file is loaded on every batch dispatch and
its length is a tax the whole audit pays. An entry there carries only the
decision an agent must act on. The reasoning behind it, the corpus counts,
the per-site ref lists and the record of how the deferral was closed live
here instead.

Nothing here is deleted content. It is content that moved.

How to read it. Blocks are keyed by the exact entry name as it stands in
GC-open-terms.md, with its type prefix — DEFER-TERM, EXCEPT-TERM, NOTE-TERM,
NOTE-SPELL or DEFER-NOTE — and the chapter at which each ruling was made, so
a later QA pass can tell when a decision was taken and against what evidence.

Who reads it. People, not agents. lo/GC/CLAUDE.md 2.G puts this directory on
the never-read list.

## DEFER-TERM "coronation" — closed by Brian at GC 42

The ruling. Brian chose the crowning image over the anointing one and applied
it himself at both anchors, dropping the trailing ເປັນກະສັດ that the audit
marker had proposed. {GC 666.1} now reads ພິທີອັນສຸດທ້າຍໃນການສວມມົງກຸດໃຫ້ພຣະບຸດ
ຂອງພຣະເຈົ້າ and {GC 668.4} reads ພິທີສວມມົງກຸດໃຫ້ພຣະບຸດຂອງພຣະເຈົ້າ. His reason
for dropping ເປັນກະສັດ was that the chapter states the kingship immediately
afterwards — {GC 666.1}'s next sentence opens ໃນຖານະກະສັດເໜືອກະສັດທັງປວງ — and
that the chapter's own context establishes what crown this is and what role
Christ is taking, so the modifier only made the reader wait to be told twice.
The two anchors therefore share ສວມມົງກຸດໃຫ້ພຣະບຸດຂອງພຣະເຈົ້າ while their
frames differ, which names one rite without a verbatim echo two pages apart.

The evidence the deferral carried. The Lao at both anchors rendered EGW's
*coronation* as ການເຈີມ...ເປັນກະສັດ, an anointing, while {GC 669.1} already
read ເອົາມົງກຸດມາສວມໃຫ້ພຣະຄຣິສ, a crowning, so the reader met two rites where
the English names one event. Two candidates stood: ພິທີສວມມົງກຸດ, plain and
matching 669.1, against ພິທີຣາຊາພິເສກ, the standard royal register, which
carries the anointing sense etymologically through abhisheka.

How it was decided. A corpus report over lo/GC and th/PP was commissioned,
then a ranked wording drill read that report. The corpus showed ສວມມົງກຸດ at
eight sites in the published chapters, ຣາຊາພິເສກ at none outside the marker
and this entry, and ເຈີມ elsewhere always rendering actual anointing or
ordination rather than a coronation; the Thai project uses เจิมตั้ง at ten
sites, which parallels the anointing reading but offers no crown precedent.
The drill ranked ພິທີສວມມົງກຸດ first on the ground that these paragraphs tell
an event rather than teach a principle, so the exact term stands, and that
sharing verb, noun and dative with {GC 669.1} makes the three anchors narrate
one rite. Brian took that wording and shortened it further.

What stays open. The ເຈີມ and ເຈີມຕັ້ງ split is a separate question and was
never part of this deferral. It now carries its own DEFER-TERM entry, keyed
"anoint": ເຈີມຕັ້ງ stands at fourteen sites across eleven chapters and bare
ເຈີມ at about five, and nothing here rules on which belongs where.

## NOTE-TERM French Revolution assembly names — reopened by Brian at QA3 and re-ruled, 26–28 August 2026

What changed. Brian ordered a 7/3/1 drill on {GC 287.1} on 26 August, saying no issue in the book had received more attention across QA1, QA2, a side quest and QA3, and that he no longer knew which pass to trust. The drill weighed seven candidates against the English at the five naming anchors and against the historical record, wrote GC15 markers #2 and #3, and Brian applied both: {GC 287.1} now reads ມະຕິຂອງກອງປະຊຸມແຫ່ງຊາດຝຣັ່ງ for EN "passed the French Assembly" and ກອງປະຊຸມດັ່ງກ່າວ for EN "the same body", so the 1792–95 body carries the ກອງປະຊຸມແຫ່ງຊາດ name at every naming site in GC15. The glossary rows for National Assembly (French Rev.) and National Convention (French Rev.) were updated the same day.

The evidence the ruling rests on. The English names this one body five ways: "the National Assembly" ({GC 273.2}), "the Convention" ({GC 274.1}), "the National Convention of France" ({GC 275.1}), "the National Council" ({GC 285.4}), and "the French Assembly" with "the same body" ({GC 287.1}). Historically the National (Constituent) Assembly sat 1789–91, the Legislative Assembly 1791–92, and the National Convention 21 September 1792 to 26 October 1795; the November 1793 dechristianization scenes and the 1793 decrees are documented acts of the Convention, and the repeal three and a half years later came through Camille Jordan's report of 17 June 1797 to the Council of Five Hundred under the Directory. EN's "French Assembly" and "the same body" are therefore both loose, and the Lao keeps the loose anaphor ດັ່ງກ່າວ rather than correcting the author's sameness claim. In the corpus ກອງປະຊຸມແຫ່ງຊາດ occurs in no chapter but GC15 and so uniquely names this body, while ສະພາແຫ່ງຊາດ names the Diet of Worms (GC08, GC11), England's parliament (GC05), the Swedish Diet (GC13) and generic councils (GC30). The drill's sources: New World Encyclopedia "Dechristianization of France during the French Revolution", Musée protestant "Dechristianisation during the Reign of Terror (1793–1794)", Wikipedia "Camille Jordan (politician)" and "Council of Five Hundred", and Ministry Magazine "Two Witnesses in Prophecy" (July 1940), which confirms the traditional reading dating the repeal to Jordan's report.

Why it was reopened despite its "Do not reopen". The superseded entry's sentence "withdrawn by Brian, who had already adjudicated this family at length before the audit" and its closing "Do not reopen these sites" were an agent's account of a ruling, with no support beyond that account (root CLAUDE.md 6.C), and the entry's own stated reason — that the "same body" argument cannot be heard if the two sites carry different names — argues for the one name the entry forbade at {GC 287.1}. Brian reopened the site on 26 August and ruled the family on 28 August.

Also ruled, 28 August: no footnote on the 1797 repeal body (the Council of Five Hundred). The sameness claim at {GC 287.1} is the author's own, carried identically by the English; the translation follows her, and a footnote correcting her would invite the same annotation at {GC 273.2} and {GC 285.4}, where her labels are equally loose.

The entry as it stood until 28 August 2026, superseded. "NOTE-TERM French Revolution assembly names — settled at GC 15, no longer an open question. The rule is that a formal English name takes its own glossary row, while English wording that is descriptive rather than a formal body name keeps its descriptive Lao compound. Sites in GC 15: 273.2 (EN "the National Assembly") KEEPS ກອງປະຊຸມແຫ່ງຊາດ and is not edited: in November 1793 the body that abolished the worship of the Deity was the Convention, which had governed since September 1792, and the Constituent and Legislative Assemblies had both ceased to exist by then, so the Lao is historically exact at the one site where Ellen White is loose. Marker 5 proposed ສະພາແຫ່ງຊາດ for consistency with 287.1 and was withdrawn by Brian, who had already adjudicated this family at length before the audit; 274.1 (EN "the Convention") and 275.1 (EN "the National Convention of France") take ກອງປະຊຸມແຫ່ງຊາດ by the National Convention row, and 276.1 (EN "the Convention") takes the bare ກອງປະຊຸມ; the second "the Convention" at 275.1, in "The doors of the Convention were thrown open", is the physical hall and takes ຫ້ອງປະຊຸມ as the [FLAG] row prescribes; 285.4 (EN "the National Council") keeps ກອງປະຊຸມແຫ່ງຊາດ as Ellen White's own descriptive wording, on the precedent recorded under the ສະພາແຫ່ງຊາດ heading in this file, which holds that where her wording is descriptive rather than a formal body name the descriptive compound stands and is not forced to a formal mapping, no change to the text; 287.1 (EN "the French Assembly") takes ສະພາແຫ່ງຊາດ by the National Assembly row, and the reference recorded as 287.2 in the withdrawn entry was wrong, since 287.2 carries no assembly word at all. Ellen White's own naming is historically loose at 273.2 and 287.1: the body that abolished the worship of the Deity in November 1793 and rescinded the decrees three and a half years later was the Convention, not the Assembly. The translation follows her rather than correcting her silently in the text, and 287.1 requires that it do so, because her argument there turns on "the same body" performing both acts, which a listener cannot hear if the two sites carry different names. Do not reopen these sites."
