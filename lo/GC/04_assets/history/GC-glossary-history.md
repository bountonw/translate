# GC glossary — decision history

Why this file exists. Every agent in the GC audit loads GC-glossary.txt on
every dispatch, so a word in a row is a word paid for on every dispatch of
every chapter, forever. A row therefore carries only what an agent must DO.
The evidence behind it — the corpus counts, the per-site ref lists, the
reasoning, and the record of when Brian decided it — lives here instead.

Nothing here is deleted content. It is content that moved.

How to read it. Blocks are keyed by the exact English head as it stands in
GC-glossary.txt, so a head grepped there finds its history here in one step.
No row in the glossary cites a path to this file: at 500 rows a repeated
path costs more than the prose it replaced, and it invites an agent to come
and load it.

Who reads it. People, not agents. lo/GC/CLAUDE.md 2.G puts this directory on
the never-read list, and that is what keeps these words off every dispatch.
Consult it for a question, a deep dive, or a later QA pass over decisions
already made.

## Christendom — ວົງການຄຣິສຕຽນ added by Brian at GC 22

The ruling. Brian confirmed that the text at GC 396.2 stands and directed
that ວົງການຄຣິສຕຽນ be added to the row with its sense stated, so that the
form is limited to the religious world spoken of as a sphere and does not
become a general option for the row's other seven forms, which all describe
the countries that profess Christianity.

The evidence. The form stands 11 times across 7 chapters: GC22 396.2,
GC25 450.1, GC27 464.1 and 478.1, GC33 547.3, GC35 574.1, GC36 584.1, 586.2,
589.2 and 592.2, and GC39 615.2. Checked against the English at every site,
only three render *Christendom* — GC22 396.2, GC25 450.1 and GC39 615.2 —
while the other eight render *the Christian world*, which has no row of its
own. Brian noted independently that he had used the term at one site where
the context better fitted a different glossary entry, and that distribution
is consistent with it: the form's ordinary work in this corpus is *Christian
world*, and GC 396.2 is one of the three places it carries *Christendom*.

How it was settled. Brian ruled that *Christian world* may have a row of its
own, on the condition that the forms already standing in the Christendom row
are not removed, since they are synonyms and pass. A Christian world row was
therefore added naming ວົງການຄຣິສຕຽນ and carrying the eight sites above, and
the Christendom row was left intact, keeping ວົງການຄຣິສຕຽນ among its forms
with the note that limits it there to the religious sphere. ໂລກຄຣິສຕຽນ stands
on both rows: Brian ruled it an option under *Christian world* as well, so the
two heads share it. Its only site so far is GC21 382.2, which renders
*Christendom*, so the form is attested under that head and approved under the
other; it is an option on the Christian world row rather than a record of use
there.

## Midnight Cry — ສຽງຮ້ອງປະກາດຍາມທ່ຽງຄືນ ruled by Brian at GC 22

The ruling. The row previously read ສຽງຮ້ອງຍາມທ່ຽງຄືນ. Brian ruled that both
the glossary and the text take ສຽງຮ້ອງປະກາດຍາມທ່ຽງຄືນ, so the row was changed
and the chapter's three quoted-name sites were brought to it: the subheading
at 398.2, the quoted name in the body of 398.2, and 408.1. The site at 400.1
already read the ruled form, so marker #7 was withdrawn rather than applied.

The anaphor. Brian asked that an anaphor be raised as an issue rather than
assumed. The fourth site, at 402.1, is not the quoted name in the English at
all: that sentence reads "In the providence of God this proclamation united
with the second angel's message", so the English points back rather than
naming. Marker #10 was rewritten to propose ຄຳປະກາດນີ້, which stands 7 times
elsewhere in the corpus. Brian declined the anaphor and applied the full name
at that site as well, so all four GC 22 sites now carry the ruled form and the
row records no short anaphoric variant.

A correction to this row. The Notes cell first written here read "Sole form;
never mark", which was wrong and was Claude's wording, not Brian's. A blanket
"never mark" defeats the purpose of a row, which exists so that the pre-pass
checks its sites; the row now reads "[CHECK] Mark any other form", following
the Mercy seat row at line 87, which reserves "Never mark" for one named
exempt site rather than using it as a blanket.

## Lord of hosts — three forms ruled to stand by Brian at GC 29

The ruling. Brian ruled on 17 August that the Lao forms of this epithet stand
as they are rather than being normalised to one, because they come from
different Bible versions, and directed that all three be added to the
glossary.

A correction to that ruling, made the same day. The row first written here
carried only two of the three, and told agents that ພຣະເຈົ້າອົງຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່
was wrong for this head because the corpus uses it for "Almighty". That was
Claude's inference from where the form happens to stand, and Brian overruled
it: the form belongs under this head because the translation avoids the four
Hebrew letters of the divine name and follows the Thai 1971 version's way of
handling them, which renders the name as "God" rather than transliterating
it. The form therefore serves both heads, and the reason is a translation
policy that no amount of corpus counting would have revealed. It is the
second time on this one row that a corpus pattern was mistaken for a rule.

The evidence. English "Lord of hosts" and its variants occur 13 times in
lo/GC/00_source/. Nine of those anchors carry an epithet built on
ຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່, and the divine name in front of it tracks the version the
quotation cites, which is the fact Brian's ruling rests on.

    ພຣະເຈົ້າຢາເວອົງຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່   8 sites   GC 424.3, 425.3, 471.1,
                                                  503.3, 633.2, 634.1,
                                                  638.1, 673.1
    ພຣະເຢໂຮວາຜູ້ຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່      1 site    GC 23.2
    ພຣະເຈົ້າອົງຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່       0 sites   approved for this head but not
                                                  yet used at one; it stands at
                                                  GC 638.1 and 676.4 for "Almighty"

Five anchors stand outside the three forms. Brian ruled on every one of them
on 17 August, and the governing principle he gave is that the titles of God
take long or short forms according to context, so a shorter form is not a
truncation to be repaired and readability decides. Four of the five are
therefore correct as they stand and must never be marked again.

GC 626.3 has the short ພຣະເຈົ້າອົງຊົງຣິດ where the English is "Lord of hosts".
Ruled acceptable as it stands.

GC 94.3 has the same short form for "Lord of Sabaoth", which the row at line
36 carries. Ruled acceptable, and the earlier reading of it as a truncated
version of the long form was wrong.

GC 156.2 carries the honorific pronoun ພຣະອົງ and no title. Ruled correct:
"We are writing a book, not a dissertation in lexicography. Sentences have to
be readable."

GC 301.3 supplies the fuller ອົງພຣະຜູ້ເປັນເຈົ້າອົງຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່ inside a
quotation, where Ellen White's English carries only the pronoun "He" and the
title is Isaiah 28:5's own. Ruled correct because it stands in a Bible verse,
which is not ours to normalise.

GC 529.1 is the one live site. It renders no epithet at all, using the plain
ອົງພຣະຜູ້ເປັນເຈົ້າ to frame a Zechariah 4:6 quotation. Brian directed that it be
left unmarked if that chapter's own audit run will catch it, and the row was
given its [CHECK] tag so that the run does.

A caution for a later pass. The bare epithet ອົງຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່ with no
divine name in front of it is a third and unrelated job for the same words:
it renders "the Most High" at GC 503.3 and GC 659.2 and "Power" at GC 643.1.
Neither row covers it and neither should.

How the evidence was gathered. A first corpus report claimed no Lao rendering
of the title existed anywhere, which was wrong; it had spelling-checked two
guessed forms rather than searching. A second claimed nine Lao sites and
missed four. Both were caught by greping the shared substring ຊົງຣິດ across
lo/GC/03_public/ and reading every hit, which is the method a later pass
should use rather than searching for a form it expects to find.

## Almighty (divine title) — ພຣະເຈົ້າອົງຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່ given its own head at GC 29

Why the row exists. The form renders "the Almighty" at GC 638.1, quoting
Isaiah 13:6, and "Lord God Almighty" at GC 676.4, quoting Revelation 21:22,
so it needs a head of its own. It also renders "Lord of hosts" and is listed
under that head as well, by Brian's ruling of 17 August recorded in the block
above: the translation avoids the four Hebrew letters of the divine name and
follows the Thai 1971 version in doing so, which is why one Lao form serves
two English titles here.

The evidence, and what it does not prove. At GC 638.1 both forms stand in the
same paragraph: ພຣະເຈົ້າຢາເວອົງຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່ renders "the day of the Lord
of hosts" from Isaiah 2:12, and ພຣະເຈົ້າອົງຊົງຣິດອຳນາດຍິ່ງໃຫຍ່ renders "a
destruction from the Almighty" from Isaiah 13:6. That paragraph was read as
proof that the manuscript keeps the two titles strictly apart, and the first
version of these rows was built on that reading. It proves only that both
forms are available at one site; the choice between them at any given site
follows the version cited, not the English head.
