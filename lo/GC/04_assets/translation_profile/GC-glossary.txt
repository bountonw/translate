<!-- Tag [PROVISIONAL], introduced by Brian. A PROVISIONAL row records a term that is NOT attested in this corpus: it exists so another project mining this glossary inherits a term built on GC's settled patterns, and it makes no claim about this book. Four consequences bind every agent that reads this table. A provisional row is never a finding. Its absence from the manuscript is never reported. The batch pre-pass never treats it as a missing mapping. No manuscript site is ever edited to match it. A provisional row becomes an ordinary row only when Brian rules that it does, once the term actually appears in the text; no agent promotes one on its own, and no agent demotes an ordinary row to provisional. Where a provisional row's Lao is composed from attested pieces rather than found whole, its Notes say which pieces and where they are attested, so the reader can see what is evidence and what is construction. -->
<!-- Term-family policy, decided by Brian. Where one English term has several Lao renderings, the variation is accepted unless there is a positive argument for narrowing it: several renderings are not a defect on their face, and the burden of proof runs toward narrowing, not toward variation. Where a family's forms are generated compositionally, approve the pattern and list attested forms as examples, because an exhaustive whitelist will flag the next form built the same way and the family never closes. A row must never both accept variation and forbid a form the manuscript actually uses; that contradiction, and not the translation, is what makes a batch pre-pass report an established rendering as a missing mapping. A deferred family is closed decisively — either every site is fixed, or each site is ruled to warrant its own reading — and is never left open to be re-flagged in every chapter that meets it. See the Romanist and Papist rows for a worked example. -->

<!-- Script in running text, decided by Brian. Most Lao readers do not read English, so running text must be readable as Lao throughout: the Lao form stands first and the English follows in parentheses wherever the original has to be preserved. Bare Latin script inside a paragraph is a defect however accurate the English is, because a reader who cannot decode it loses that piece of the sentence. Footnotes are the one exception, being consulted rather than read: a cited periodical or newspaper title is printed there exactly as in English. This governs every name and term, not only the cited titles the periodical row covers. -->

| Dark Ages | ຍຸກມືດ / ສະໄໝຍຸກມືດ | [CHECK] Both forms accepted; neither is an error where it stands. Counts verified by grep: English "dark ages" 6 occurrences; Lao ຍຸກມືດ 16, of which 12 are inside ສະໄໝຍຸກມືດ, leaving 4 where the bare form stands alone; ສະໄໝຍຸກມືດ 12. ສະໄໝຍຸກມືດ is the running-prose form and is the default for new and edited work. The counts suggest that the fuller form is a first mention and the bare form its anaphor, and the sites do not bear that out, so no such rule is recorded: three of the four bare uses are display text — the GC03 chapter title ຍຸກມືດຝ່າຍຈິດວິນຍານ, the section heading between GC 54.2 and GC 55.1, and the section heading between GC 570.2 and GC 571.1 — and in GC03 the bare heading stands before the full prose form rather than after it. The fourth bare use is prose, GC 577.3, "amid the gloom of the Dark Ages", in a chapter where the full form already stands at GC 563.2, GC 571.1 and GC 572.3; anaphora is possible there but is not the governing rule.
